Weddings guide

Wedding planning timeline: 12 months out to the week of

By Ivan Dummett · Updated November 18, 2026

Build your timeline with the free wedding countdownFree 12-month wedding checklist

There is no single correct order for planning a wedding — a courthouse ceremony and a 200-guest weekend need very different lead times. But most plans follow a similar shape: big decisions and popular vendors first, details in the middle, logistics at the end. Use this timeline as a menu. Skip what doesn't apply.

If your date is closer than 12 months, start at the section that matches and combine the earlier steps.

12 months out (or as soon as you're engaged)

  • Agree on a rough budget and who is contributing.
  • Draft a guest list range — it drives the venue size and most costs.
  • Pick a season or a short list of dates.
  • Tour and book the ceremony and reception venues.
  • Book the vendors that tend to fill up first in your area, often the photographer and the officiant.

9 to 11 months out

  • Book catering if the venue doesn't include it.
  • Choose music: band, DJ, or a playlist plan.
  • Start looking at attire, since orders and alterations can take months.
  • Set up a wedding website if you want one, and decide how you'll collect RSVPs.
  • If many guests are traveling, reserve a hotel room block.

6 to 8 months out

  • Send save-the-dates, especially for destination or holiday-weekend dates.
  • Book florist, rentals, hair and makeup, and transportation.
  • Plan the ceremony with your officiant.
  • Start a gift registry if you plan to have one.

3 to 5 months out

  • Order invitations. Many couples mail them six to eight weeks before the date.
  • Schedule a menu tasting and a hair and makeup trial.
  • Buy rings and plan the first alteration fitting.
  • Check what your location requires for a marriage license and when you can apply.

1 to 2 months out

  • Mail invitations, then track RSVPs as they come in.
  • Confirm details and arrival times with every vendor.
  • Write vows or readings.
  • Draft the seating plan once most RSVPs are back.
  • Apply for the marriage license within the window your location allows.

The final week

  • Give final numbers to the caterer and venue.
  • Share a day-of schedule with the wedding party and vendors.
  • Pack an emergency kit: safety pins, stain remover, phone chargers, snacks.
  • Prepare final payments and tips in labeled envelopes.
  • Pick one person to be the point of contact on the day so you don't have to be.

Keep everything in one place

The biggest source of stress is usually scattered information — quotes in email, guest addresses in a text thread, the budget in your head. Pick one home for it, whether that's a printed binder, a spreadsheet, or both.

Frequently asked questions

Can you plan a wedding in six months?

Many people do. Book the venue and the photographer right away, and keep the guest list and decor simple.

When should invitations go out?

Many couples mail them six to eight weeks before the date. Destination and holiday dates usually go out earlier.
