Family guide

Chore chart ideas by age, from 3 to 12

By Ivan Dummett · Updated November 18, 2026

Make a free printable chore chart

A chore chart works best when the jobs fit the child: small enough to finish, real enough to matter. Below are ideas many families use at different ages. Every child is different, so treat these as a menu, not a rulebook — you know your kid best.

Ages 3–4: helping alongside you

  • Put toys back in a bin
  • Put dirty clothes in the hamper
  • Help feed a pet (you measure, they pour)
  • Wipe up small spills with a cloth
  • Carry a plastic plate to the counter

At this age, the chart is mostly pictures. A sticker for each finished job is plenty.

Ages 5–6: simple solo jobs

  • Make the bed (it doesn't need to be neat)
  • Set the table with napkins and spoons
  • Match socks from the laundry
  • Water plants with a small watering can
  • Get dressed and put pajamas away

Ages 7–9: routines and small responsibilities

  • Pack a school bag the night before
  • Empty small trash cans
  • Fold and put away their own laundry
  • Unload the silverware from the dishwasher
  • Sweep a small area or use a handheld vacuum
  • Help make a simple lunch

Ages 10–12: real household jobs

  • Load or unload the whole dishwasher
  • Run a load of laundry with you nearby
  • Take bins to the curb
  • Clean the bathroom sink and mirror
  • Help plan and cook one meal a week
  • Look after a pet for the week: food, water, and cleanup

Making the chart work

Keep it short

Three to five daily jobs is usually plenty. A long chart becomes a list of things that didn't get done.

Teach the job before it goes on the chart

Do it together a few times first. "Clean your room" means something different to a seven-year-old than it does to you, so break it into steps: clothes in the hamper, books on the shelf, toys in the bin.

Decide what the checkmarks are for

Some families tie chores to allowance, some use stars toward a small reward, and some keep chores as "what everyone does to help." Any of these can work. Just pick one, explain it clearly, and stick with it.

Review it every few months

Kids grow fast. Swap jobs that have become too easy for something new, and let older kids help choose.
