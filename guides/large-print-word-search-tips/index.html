Large print word searches: printing tips and how to make your own

By Ivan Dummett · Updated November 18, 2026

Make a free custom word searchFree 3-puzzle large print sample

Word searches are a popular, low-pressure puzzle for all ages, and large-print versions are easier on the eyes for many readers. Whether you're printing puzzles for yourself, a parent, or an activity group, a few details make a big difference.

What makes a word search "large print"

  • Bigger letters and cells. A 15 × 15 grid on a letter-size page gives each letter a lot more room than the 20 × 20 grids in many newsstand books.
  • A clean, even font. A monospaced font keeps every column lined up, so diagonal words are easier to follow.
  • Clear grid lines. Light lines between cells help your eye stay in a row without crowding the letters.
  • A readable word list. The list should be as easy to read as the grid, with a checkbox for each word.
  • Answer keys with the words highlighted, so nobody gets stuck on the last one.

Printing so the letters stay big

  1. In the print dialog, choose Actual size or 100%. "Fit to page" can shrink the grid.
  2. Print in black on plain white paper for the most contrast. Bright white paper works better than recycled gray stock.
  3. For a clipboard or lap use, slightly heavier paper keeps the page from curling.
  4. Print only the puzzles you need. You don't have to print the whole book at once.

Making the puzzle easier or harder

If you make your own puzzle, these settings change the difficulty:

  • Directions: forward and down only is the easiest. Adding diagonals and backwards words makes it much harder.
  • Grid size: fewer cells means less searching.
  • Word length: longer words are easier to spot than three-letter ones.
  • Theme: a familiar theme like a garden, a kitchen, or a favorite place helps people guess which words to look for.

Make a personal puzzle

A puzzle made from family names, a hometown, or a hobby makes a thoughtful little gift. The free word search maker lets you type your own words, choose the grid size, and print the puzzle with an answer key.

Ideas for using printed puzzles

  • A folder of puzzles for travel, waiting rooms, or a hospital visit
  • An activity for a group meeting or a family gathering
  • A quiet-time sheet for older kids next to their coloring pages
  • A tear-off pad by the coffee maker for a daily puzzle
