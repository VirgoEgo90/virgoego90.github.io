Wall art guide

Printable wall art sizes: which file fits which frame

By Ivan Dummett · Updated November 18, 2026

Check a file with the free print size & DPI guideFree 8×10 lavender botanical print

Printable wall art is one of the easiest ways to decorate on a budget, but the file has to match the frame. Otherwise the print gets cropped or ends up with white bars. This guide explains aspect ratios in plain terms and lists the pixels you need for a sharp print.

Aspect ratio comes first

An aspect ratio is the shape of the image: its width compared with its height. Frames that share a ratio can use the same file at different sizes. Frames with a different ratio need a different crop.

RatioCommon frame sizes (inches)
4:58×10, 16×20
3:46×8, 9×12, 12×16, 18×24
2:34×6, 8×12, 12×18, 20×30, 24×36
11:1411×14 (its own shape)
ISO A (1:1.414)A5, A4, A3, A2, A1

That's why most printable art comes as several files. An 8×10 file can't fill an 11×14 frame without cropping, because the shapes are different.

Pixels for a sharp print

For prints you look at up close, the usual target is 300 pixels per inch (often called 300 DPI). Multiply each side in inches by 300:

Print sizePixels at 300 DPI
5×7 in1500 × 2100
8×10 in2400 × 3000
11×14 in3300 × 4200
16×20 in4800 × 6000
24×36 in7200 × 10800
A4 (8.27×11.69 in)2480 × 3508

Large posters are usually seen from farther away, so 150–200 pixels per inch often looks fine at 24×36. For a desk-sized print, aim for 300.

Check a file before you print

Divide the image's pixel width by the print width in inches. A 3000-pixel-wide image printed 10 inches wide is 300 pixels per inch. At 20 inches wide, it's 150. The free print size and DPI guide does this for every common frame size at once.

Where to print

  • At home, up to 8.5×11 or A4, on matte photo paper or heavy card stock. Turn on the printer's highest quality setting.
  • At a print shop or photo counter, for 11×14 and larger. Upload the file that matches your frame's ratio, and turn off any auto-crop or "enhance" option.

Framing tips

  • A mat makes a small print look intentional. An 8×10 print in an 11×14 frame with an 8×10 mat opening is a classic setup.
  • For a gallery wall, keep one frame color and leave even gaps between frames, about 2 to 3 inches.
  • In bathrooms and kitchens, use a frame with glass or acrylic so steam doesn't warp the paper.
