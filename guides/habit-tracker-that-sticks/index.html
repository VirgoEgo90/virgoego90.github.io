Habits guide

How to make a habit tracker you'll actually keep using

By Ivan Dummett · Updated November 18, 2026

Make a free dated habit tracker

A habit tracker is a grid: habits down the side, days across the top, and a mark each day you do the thing. It's simple, which is why it works. It's also easy to quit. These are the choices that make one last.

Track fewer habits than you think

A row of fifteen habits looks motivating on day one and discouraging by day ten. Start with three to five. If you're filling in every box after two weeks, add one more.

Define each habit so it's obvious when it's done

"Exercise more" is hard to check off. "Walk 15 minutes" isn't. Write each habit so it has a clear finish line:

  • "Read" → Read 10 pages
  • "Drink water" → Refill my bottle twice
  • "Be tidy" → Clear the kitchen counter before bed
  • "Save money" → No takeout on weekdays

Make the bar low enough that you could do it on a bad day. You can raise it later.

Monthly or weekly?

A monthly grid (31 columns) shows long streaks and patterns at a glance. Many people like seeing a whole month fill up. A weekly grid has more room per day and feels less heavy. If you've abandoned trackers before, start weekly. Each Monday is a fresh page.

Put it where you'll see it

A tracker in a closed notebook gets forgotten. Tape it inside a kitchen cabinet, on the fridge, or next to your bathroom mirror. Tie the check-in to something that already happens every day, like brushing your teeth at night.

Decide now what a missed day means

The most common reason people quit is a broken streak. Set a rule ahead of time. Two that work well:

  • Never miss twice. One missed day is normal. Two in a row means make tomorrow's version tiny.
  • Count the month, not the streak. 20 out of 31 days is a good month, even with gaps.

Review once a week

Every week, take two minutes to look at the grid. Which habits are easy now? Which keep getting skipped? Make a skipped one smaller, or drop it. A tracker is information, not a grade.

Pair it with planning pages

Habits often stall because the setup is missing. A meal plan makes "cook at home" possible. A grocery list makes "pack lunch" possible. That's why the paid tracker pack puts habit grids next to meal plans, grocery lists, and simple logs. Those pages are trackers, not diet or fitness advice.
