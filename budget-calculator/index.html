This calculator only splits a number you type into percentages. It is not financial advice, a recommendation, or a plan for your situation. The 50/30/20 rule is a common starting point, not a requirement. “Take-home pay” here means the amount that reaches your bank account after tax and payroll deductions.

How to use a 50/30/20 monthly budget

  1. Look up your monthly take-home pay — what’s left after tax and other payroll deductions. If you are paid weekly or every two weeks, multiply a typical paycheck by 52 or 26, then divide by 12.
  2. Leave the split at 50/30/20, or change the percentages so they still add up to 100. A higher “needs” share is common when housing takes up more of a paycheck.
  3. Needs are the bills you have to pay to live and work: housing, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, childcare, and minimum loan payments. Wants are flexible spending such as restaurants, streaming, and hobbies. The last share is for savings and any extra debt payments above the minimum.
  4. If you like, enter what you actually spent last month to see where you were over or under the target amounts.
